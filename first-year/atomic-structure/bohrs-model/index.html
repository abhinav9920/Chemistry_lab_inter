Calculation

Energy absorbed by the electron

The energy of an electron in the nth orbit of hydrogen is:

En = −13.6 / n² eV

E₁ = −13.600 eV
E₂ = −3.400 eV

|ΔE| = 13.6 | 1/nlower² − 1/nupper² | eV

|ΔE| = 10.200 eV

E = hν = hc/λ

ν = 2.466 × 10¹⁵ Hz
λ = 121.50 nm

During absorption, the photon energy must exactly equal the difference between the two permitted energy levels. The electron cannot remain between the permitted orbits.

Important Ideas Demonstrated

  • Electrons can occupy only certain permitted energy levels.
  • An electron moves to a higher orbit only by absorbing a photon having exactly the required energy.
  • An excited electron is unstable and can return to a lower orbit.
  • When the electron returns to a lower orbit, a photon is emitted.
  • The photon energy is equal to the difference between the initial and final electron energy levels.
  • The wavelength of an emission line determines whether the radiation is ultraviolet, visible or infrared.
